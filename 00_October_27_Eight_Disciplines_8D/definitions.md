# Eight Disciplines (8D) Definition

## Overview

The **Eight Disciplines (8D)** methodology is a structured, team-based problem-solving approach used to identify, correct, and eliminate the root causes of defects, nonconformities, or recurring issues. Originally developed by the U.S. Department of Defense and popularized by Ford Motor Company in the 1980s, 8D has become a standard problem-solving framework in manufacturing, automotive, aerospace, and other quality-critical industries.

The methodology follows eight sequential disciplines (steps), preceded by a planning stage (D0), that guide a cross-functional team from initial problem identification through corrective action, verification, and prevention of recurrence.

## The Eight Disciplines

| Discipline | Name | Purpose |
|------------|------|---------|
| **D0** | Preparation & Emergency Response | Assess the problem, determine if 8D is appropriate, and take immediate containment or safety actions |
| **D1** | Form the Team | Assemble a cross-functional team with the product/process knowledge, authority, and skills to solve the problem |
| **D2** | Describe the Problem | Define the problem in quantifiable terms using the **5W2H** method (Who, What, When, Where, Why, How, How Many) |
| **D3** | Interim Containment Actions (ICA) | Implement temporary measures to isolate the problem from the customer until a permanent fix is in place |
| **D4** | Root Cause Analysis & Escape Points | Identify all potential root causes using tools such as **5 Whys**, **Fishbone (Ishikawa) diagrams**, or **Fault Tree Analysis**, and verify the root cause through testing |
| **D5** | Choose & Verify Permanent Corrective Actions (PCA) | Select the best long-term corrective actions, validate them through pilot testing or simulation, and confirm they will eliminate the root cause |
| **D6** | Implement & Validate Permanent Corrective Actions | Deploy the chosen corrective actions in full production, monitor results, and verify effectiveness over time |
| **D7** | Prevent Recurrence | Update standards, procedures, training, specifications, and management systems (e.g., FMEAs, control plans) to prevent the problem or similar problems from recurring |
| **D8** | Recognize the Team | Acknowledge the team's efforts, document lessons learned, and formally close the 8D process |

---

**Note**: The 8D methodology continues to evolve. Current best practices emphasize digital 8D tracking systems, integration with quality management software, and alignment with IATF 16949 requirements for automotive suppliers.
